At a glance

Know instantly what’s already yours.

Every poster carries its status, checked against Plex, Jellyfin, Sonarr and Radarr: on Discover, in search results, across an actor’s filmography and a studio’s whole catalog. No more opening three apps to find out you already have it.

Owned
In Plex or Jellyfin, or already on disk through Radarr or Sonarr.
Downloading
In the download queue now, or a series that’s only partly on disk.
Missing
Monitored and released, but nothing on disk yet.
Coming soon
Tracked but not out yet, so it never looks like something’s missing.
No badge
Not in your library. Add it in one click, or request it.

Open any title for the rest: every season with each episode’s have-it-or-missing status, cast and crew, franchises and “More like this”, and the file itself, down to its location, size, resolution, codecs, HDR and audio.

Getting around

Every section, one click away.

After the Plex app on Apple TV: a small frosted rail floats at the left edge with your photo and every section, grouped by hairlines. One click goes straight there, and resting on an icon shows its name.

Room for the page
Nothing sits beside the page any more, so a title’s backdrop runs right to the edge of the window.
The same everywhere
The website, the Mac app and the Windows app share the rail and the menu, frosted glass and all.
Your photo, on your server
Add one in Settings › Account and it tops the rail and shows next to each household member. It’s stripped of location and camera details and never leaves your Marquee server.

How it connects

It sits in front of the stack you already run.

Marquee doesn’t replace Plex, Jellyfin, Sonarr or Radarr. Connect whichever you use from Settings → Integrations and it keeps them in step: your libraries sync every hour, and Sonarr and Radarr webhooks report each grab and finished download as it happens.

Notifications, from your own server.

After you sign in, Marquee asks whether this device should get notifications: a request approved or declined, a title ready to watch. Your server sends them itself, so there’s no third-party service to sign up for and no API key to paste in.

Sealed
On the website they’re standard Web Push, encrypted end to end so only your device can read them. The browser’s own push relay carries the sealed message and nothing more.
On your phone
Open Marquee over https, for example through a tunnel or reverse proxy. On iPhone and iPad, add it to the Home Screen first.
No relay at all
The Mac and Windows apps get them straight from your server, over a live connection.
Your devices
Settings › Account › Notifications turns them on or off, lists your devices and sends a test.
Trusted members skip the line
Auto-approve one member’s movie requests, TV requests, or both, and they go straight to Radarr or Sonarr.
Bring a watchlist
Paste a public Trakt list or watchlist, and anything you don’t already have or haven’t requested joins the queue.
Tell the whole house
Grabs, finished downloads, approvals and declines can also post to a Discord channel, an ntfy topic or any webhook.

Marquee for Mac

Your server, in a native window.

A real SwiftUI app with no library of its own. It finds the Marquee server on your home network by itself, signs in with a token kept in your Keychain, and shows exactly what the website shows. On a phone, the website works just as well.

  • Notifications straight from your server. System banners and a Dock badge, over a live connection with no relay in between. Click one and the title opens, even if the app was closed.
  • The same rail, and your photo. Every section one click from the left edge, as on the website, with your profile photo on top.
  • Right-click everything. Open, Add or Request, Favorite, Copy Link and Open in Browser on any poster or person.

Open it and drag Marquee onto the Applications folder beside it. It isn’t notarized yet, so the first launch needs System Settings › Privacy & Security › Open Anyway. After that, it updates itself.

Marquee for Windows Preview

And now, on Windows.

A native WinUI 3 app, built alongside the Mac one: the same menu and the same pages, talking to the same server. Type in your server’s address, sign in, and the household’s library is there.

  • Everything the house uses. Discover, Movies and Series, search, title pages with Add or Request, favorites, the calendar, and requests with their decline reasons.
  • Notifications with no relay. While Marquee is open, your server’s notifications show up as Windows notifications, straight over a live connection.
  • Run the household from it. Add, edit and remove members, and give each one a photo.

Not there yet: integrations settings (use the website for now), marquee:// links, and finding your server on the network by itself.

Download for Windows Build instructions →

Run it: it installs Marquee for your account (no admin prompt), adds it to the Start menu and opens it. It isn’t code-signed yet, so if SmartScreen says “Windows protected your PC”, choose More info › Run anyway. A newer installer updates it in place.

Needs Windows 10 1809 or later, or Windows 11, and a Marquee server on 0.22.0 or newer; photos and notifications need 0.29.0.

Runs on your server

One container. Your hardware.

Marquee is a single Docker image with Postgres built in, so there’s nothing else to install or wire up. Run it on Unraid, a Synology or a spare box, next to the Plex, Jellyfin, Sonarr and Radarr it talks to. Database migrations apply on every start.

Docker Compose

$ git clone https://github.com/TimmyAmant/marquee.git
$ cd marquee
$ cp .env.local.example .env
# now set POSTGRES_PASSWORD, AUTH_SECRET and
# MASTER_ENCRYPTION_KEY in .env
$ docker compose up -d

Then open http://<your-server-ip>:3000. The first visit creates your admin account; connect everything else from Settings → Integrations.

Generate the two secrets with openssl rand -base64 32, and back up MASTER_ENCRYPTION_KEY: it’s what decrypts your saved Plex, Jellyfin, Sonarr and Radarr credentials. You’ll also want a free TMDb API key, in .env or added later in the app.

Away from home

Marquee speaks plain HTTP on your network. The README walks through a Cloudflare Tunnel on your own domain, which also brings the https that notifications on phones need, and putting Authelia or Cloudflare Access in front, with what to exempt so the apps and webhooks keep working.

Remote access guide

Your library stays on your server.

No cloud
No account with us, no hosted service and no analytics.
Your data
What you own, what the house asked for, who they are and their profile photos live in a Postgres database inside the container, which doesn’t listen outside it.
Your keys
Saved Plex, Jellyfin, Sonarr and Radarr credentials are encrypted at rest with AES-256-GCM. Passwords are hashed with argon2 and sign-ins are rate-limited.
Calls out
Only to what you connect: TMDb for metadata and artwork, plus TheTVDB, Trakt, plex.tv and your notification channels if you use them. Notifications in a browser pass through that browser’s push relay, sealed so it can’t read them.

Marquee is in beta and changes often. Back up your database before updating.
